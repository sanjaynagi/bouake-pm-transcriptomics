## 0. Points needing the author's judgement (read first)

1. **"ABCH transporters occur only in arthropods" is not strictly correct.** Annilo et al. 2006 report an ABCH-subfamily gene in zebrafish (abstract: "ABCH subfamily gene found only in zebrafish"). Safer wording: "absent from mammals, present in arthropods (and some fish)".
2. **Kefi et al. 2023 (ABCH2, *An. coluzzii*) partly cuts against the CHC mechanism you describe.** Silencing ABCH2 raised deltamethrin mortality and penetration (consistent with ABCH slowing uptake), but leg CHC content was "no affect by ABCH2 silencing", and the authors favour a direct deltamethrin-pumping role. The CHC-deposition function is from other insects (Locusta/Tribolium ABCH-9C). I suggest citing Kefi 2023 explicitly and softening the sentence accordingly.
3. **Poupardin et al. 2014 is Thai *Ae. aegypti* (not Latin America).** The sentence "alpha-esterase associated with temephos resistance in *Ae. aegypti* and *Ae. albopictus* in Latin America" is wrong on geography for both: Poupardin 2014 = Thailand; Grigoraki 2015 = *Ae. albopictus* from Greece. Fix the text ("in Asia and Europe", or "in *Aedes*"). Grisales 2013 (Colombia) is the Latin American paper.
4. **Poupardin/Grigoraki "alpha-esterase"**: the genes are CCEae3a/CCEae6a (carboxylesterases). Both papers support "carboxylesterases amplified in temephos resistance"; "alpha-esterase" is plausible from orthology but is not stated in the abstracts.
5. **Grisales et al. 2013 and "Cyp6 subfamily"**: the abstract says only "Several candidate genes potentially responsible for metabolic resistance to temephos were identified". Cyp6 involvement needs checking in the full text (I did not verify it). Rated PARTIAL.
6. **Dengela 2018 only PARTLY supports "usually highly effective, with long residual performance".** Abstract: pirimiphos-methyl "ranged from 2 months to 9 months (average = 5.3 months), but reported 13 incidents of underperformance". Better supports: Sherrard-Smith 2018 and Oxborough 2014 (both verified below). Consider keeping Dengela but wording as "typically 4-6 months".
7. **Raymond et al. 2001** was inferred as the Genetica review (Culex pipiens OP resistance, gene amplification and migration). There is also Raymond et al. 1991 Nature 350:151-153 "Worldwide migration of amplified insecticide resistance genes in mosquitoes" (10.1038/350151a0, resolved on Crossref, abstract not read), which is the original on worldwide spread. Author should confirm which was intended.
8. **Djogbénou et al. 2009** inferred as the Malar J ace-1 duplication paper (fits context: duplication + OP/carbamate resistance); the 2008 PLoS ONE introgression paper was not checked. Confirm.
9. **Yunta et al. 2019, Edi et al. 2014, Sherrard-Smith 2018, Dengela 2018, Clarkson 2014/2021, Grau-Bové 2021, Oumbouke 2020, Robinson 2011, Santolamazza 2008, Poupardin 2014, Grigoraki 2015** are my inference from context, one candidate each; all exist and are topically right, but confirm they are the ones you meant.
10. **SAP2 is Ingham et al. 2020 *Nature*** (published online Dec 2019, Crossref lists year 2019 for the record, Europe PMC 2020; volume 577, 2020 issue). It shows *pyrethroid* binding/resistance, not OP sequestration; the manuscript's "insecticide sequestration" is a mild extension. The PLoS Genet 2021 Ingham paper (10.1371/journal.pgen.1009970) is NOT the SAP2 paper and does not support the claim.
11. **No peer-reviewed paper exists for the copying-model / scaffold-phasing method** (Part A item 12). See there.
12. **L995F "almost fixation"**: abstracts of Clarkson 2021 and Ag1000G 2020 do not give the frequency. Rated PARTIAL; frequency is in the full text/figures.
13. **IRS products**: best sources are peer-reviewed hut/cone trials (one per product, verified) plus the WHO prequalification list (grey literature, URL returned HTTP 200 but contents not parsed). No single peer-reviewed paper covers "Sylando"; the chlorfenapyr citation is a chlorfenapyr (± alpha-cypermethrin) IRS hut study.
14. **MalariaGEN Ag3 data citation**: see Part C. Because Ag3.10+ releases carry publication-permission terms, check that every sample set used is unrestricted (the manuscript already does this).

## 4. Part C: MalariaGEN Ag3 data citations
Sources read: Ag3.0 resource page (https://www.malariagen.net/data_package/ag30-anopheles-gambiae-data-resource/), Ag3.10 page, and release pages (SNP, haplotype, CNV) for citation text. Terms: Ag3.0 uses the Ag1000G Terms of Use (Fort Lauderdale principles: allow the Consortium first global-analysis papers; contact M. Donnelly about questions or including the Consortium as author). (haplotype and CNV releases analogous). The Ag1000G phase 2 page asks for the Genome Research 2020 paper plus Lucas 2019 for CNV data; I found no Ag3-specific resource paper in Crossref/EPMC, so the standard practice is to cite the Genome Research 2020 paper (phase 2 resource, the method reference for the pipeline and phasing), the release web citations, and for CNV, Lucas 2019. Suggested citations: Ag1000G 2020; Ag1000G 2017 (Nature, phase 1; optional); Lucas 2019 (copy number, used for the ABCH1 CNV calls); phase 3 SNP/haplotype/CNV release citations; and the malariagen_data software (Zenodo/GitHub, not verified by me).
